# CcSinglecell's successor must change the question

As of November 2026, the frontier of multi-omics biomarker discovery is a stricter way of working more than a new model: every large benchmark finds that clinical variables plus gene expression carry nearly all prognostic signal, that deep and multimodal models fail to beat clinical-favouring penalised Cox models or block forests and calibrate worse, and that the colorectal cancer (CRC) signals which demonstrably add to stage are post-operative circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA; multivariable disease-free survival hazard ratio about 12) and H&E deep-learning scores (stage-adjusted HR about 3), not mRNA or miRNA panels. CcSinglecell's ΔC ≈ −0.001 over age+stage is therefore the expected result — at 78 events the Riley sample-size criterion supports only about six predictors — and its permutation, power-curve and learning-curve harness is already more rigorous than most published work. A cutting-edge successor should keep the original idea (tumour-intrinsic single-cell biology plus miRNA regulation) but rebuild every stage: patient-replicated cell states from the 4.27-million-cell CRC atlas, carried into bulk tumours by deconvolution, instead of MAGIC and VIM-rooted pseudotime; a regulator-activity module on versioned TargetScan 8, scanMiR and TarBase v9 files instead of hoardeR scraping and target counts; clinical-favouring structured penalisation instead of blended rankings; 3-year relapse-free and progression-free intervals across GEO, trial and TCGA cohorts instead of TCGA overall survival alone; and TRIPOD+AI-grade validation with random-signature nulls. hoardeR should not be ported: CRAN archived it on 2026-04-13, the TargetScan 7.1 endpoint it hard-codes now returns HTTP 403, and its one useful function reduces to about ten lines that read a checksummed bulk file. The six gated phases below begin by porting the honesty harness, the most valuable thing CcSinglecell owns, and end with ctDNA-conditional and treatment-interaction analyses aimed at the decision clinicians actually face: who relapses among ctDNA-negative stage III patients.

## A ΔC of zero is the field's normal result, not a pipeline bug

The most important planning fact is that the old pipeline's null replicates the literature. Herrmann and colleagues compared 11 methods on 18 TCGA multi-omics datasets and found a clinical-only Cox model at a **mean Uno C of 0.618**; block forest was the only method to beat it on average, at **0.620**, paired tests found no significant difference, and in **6 of 18 datasets no omics method beat clinical Cox at all** ([Herrmann et al. 2021](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/22/3/bbaa167/5895463)). SurvBoard, running 12 models over 28 datasets, found that regularised linear and random-forest methods beat all ten deep-learning models, that clinical data and expression were the most predictive modalities, that **many models got worse as modalities were added**, and that the deep model with the best C-index was among the worst calibrated; its authors conclude multi-omics survival analysis "currently has limited clinical applications" ([SurvBoard 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12486238/)). In the TCGA colorectal cohort specifically, a clinical-only model scored **C = 0.655 ± 0.119 and beat every genomic and multimodal model** in the MMP benchmark, with fold standard deviations of 0.10 to 0.20 ([Song et al., MMP](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00224v1)). A decade earlier, four gene-expression risk scores in the PETACC-3 trial "contribute only marginally" beyond established risk factors ([Di Narzo et al. 2014](https://doc.rero.ch/record/299791/files/dju247.pdf)).

Sample-size arithmetic explains why. Applying Riley's shrinkage criterion at CcSinglecell's 22% event fraction, even an optimistic Cox–Snell R² of 0.15 needs about 275 patients (roughly 62 events) for five predictors and about 549 (roughly 123 events) for ten; at R² = 0.05, five predictors already need about 195 events ([pmsampsize](https://rdrr.io/cran/pmsampsize/man/pmsampsize.html)). With 78 events the cohort supports **about six parameters before shrinkage exceeds 10%**, so an elastic-net over 1,800 mRNA+miRNA features operates deep in heavy-shrinkage territory, and with fold standard deviations near 0.1 any molecular increment below roughly 0.03–0.05 in C is undetectable in TCGA alone. The bar for "significant" is also the wrong one: **most random gene signatures are significantly associated with outcome**, largely through correlation with proliferation ([Venet et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002240)), so a candidate must beat random and proliferation-matched nulls, not merely "no signal".

The implication is encouraging rather than deflating. CcSinglecell's Phase 4b harness — a permutation test applied only to the genomic block, a semi-synthetic power curve that bounds the minimum detectable effect, and a learning curve that separates power-limited from signal-limited nulls — already asks the questions most published biomarker papers skip, and it should seed the new repository. The successor's primary endpoint should be ΔC over a *strong* clinical baseline, with a confidence interval, pre-registered and powered through pooled and external cohorts. One honest gap remains: no 2024–2026 benchmark reporting ΔC over clinical variables for mRNA+miRNA in TCGA-COAD/READ specifically was found, and SurvBoard's per-dataset numbers sit in unread supplements.

## Signal beyond stage lives in ctDNA, H&E and immune context

Post-operative ctDNA for molecular residual disease is the CRC layer with the strongest validated prognostic evidence, but it has not yet become a reliable guide to treatment. In GALAXY's 2,240 patients, ctDNA positivity carried a **multivariable disease-free survival HR of 12.08**, against 1.56 for nodal positivity and 2.03 for BRAF V600E, and adjuvant benefit was confined to ctDNA-positive patients ([GALAXY, Nat Med 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11564113/)). Yet in DYNAMIC-III (1,002 randomised stage III patients), de-escalating ctDNA-negative patients cut oxaliplatin use from 88.6% to 34.8% while 3-year relapse-free survival (RFS) was **85.3% against 88.1%, missing the non-inferiority margin**, and escalation in ctDNA-positive patients did not help (2-year RFS 51% against 61%) ([ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/news/october-2025/postsurgical-ctdna-testing-in-stage-iii-colon-cancer-for-treatment-de-escalation/)). ESMO's coverage called ctDNA-guided therapy "not ready for prime time" ([ESMO](https://www.esmo.org/insights-by-esmo/all-news/ctdna-guided-adjuvant-chemotherapy-in-colon-cancer-not-ready-for-prime-time)), and NCCN classes ctDNA as "prognostic, but not predictive" and does not recommend de-escalation on it ([OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/nccn-updates-ctdna-stance-in-colon-rectal-and-mcc-guidelines)).

The routine H&E slide, which TCGA makes public, is the next-strongest layer. DoMore-v1-CRC gave an HR of **3.04 (2.07–4.47) after adjustment for established markers including pN stage** in 1,122 stage II/III QUASAR 2 patients ([Skrede et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32007170/)) and, combined with pathology staging, reclassified guideline risk groups ([Kleppe et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35964620/)); end-to-end attention models trained on 4,428 patients reached external disease-specific survival HRs of 2.23 to 3.08 ([Jiang et al., Lancet Digit Health](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landig/article/PIIS2589-7500(23)00208-X/fulltext)). Immunoscore remains the validated spatial benchmark — CD3/CD8 density in tumour core and invasive margin, prognostic independent of TNM ([Pagès et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30789-X)) — with an HR of 1.54 for low scores in IDEA-France, although its predictive claim for 3 versus 6 months of chemotherapy still "needs to be confirmed" ([ASCO abstract](https://ace.asco.org/content/258839/abstract/171171)). Against this, prognostic miRNA evidence is mostly small and single-marker: the pooled overall-survival HR for miR-20a has a confidence interval of 0.90–3.14, crossing 1 ([meta-analysis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6721456/)), and the best-replicated CRC miRNA, low miR-31-3p, is *predictive* of anti-EGFR benefit rather than prognostic, in cohorts with overlapping authors ([ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/issues/september-15-2014/potential-marker-for-response-to-anti-egfr-therapy-in-kras-wild-type-metastatic-colorectal-cancer/)). Even rich genomics loses to clinical detail: in MSK-CHORD, features extracted from clinical notes by natural language processing beat genomic data for overall survival, including on external data ([Jee et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39506116/)).

The unmet need has also moved. In localised mismatch-repair-deficient (dMMR) colon cancer, immunotherapy has nearly closed the gap: NICHE-2 reported 68% pathological complete response and **100% 3-year disease-free survival** ([ESMO 2024](https://oncologypro.esmo.org/meeting-resources/esmo-congress-2024/neoadjuvant-immunotherapy-in-locally-advanced-mmr-deficient-colon-cancer-3-year-disease-free-survival-from-niche-2)), and ATOMIC halved recurrence risk with adjuvant atezolizumab (HR 0.50) ([Targeted Oncology](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/atezolizumab-plus-chemotherapy-improves-dfs-in-dmmr-colon-cancer)). The open decisions sit in the mismatch-repair-proficient (pMMR) majority: the roughly 15% of ctDNA-negative, de-escalated stage III patients in DYNAMIC-III who relapsed by three years, regimen choice for ctDNA-positive patients, and treatment-by-biomarker interactions such as CMS × therapy in CALGB/SWOG 80405 (P < 0.001) ([KU Leuven](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/2798750)) and ctDNA "hyperselection" for panitumumab in PARADIGM ([Nat Med 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02791-w)). The baseline to beat is therefore T and N stage, MMR status and sidedness — plus ctDNA and Immunoscore where available — not age plus stage. The successor's primary question should be: *do patient-replicated, tumour-intrinsic cell states and regulator activities, derived from single-cell data but measured in bulk, add to stage + MMR (+ H&E) for 3-year RFS in pMMR stage II/III colon cancer across independent cohorts?* ctDNA-conditional and predictive analyses are stretch goals that require consortium data; TCGA overall survival cannot answer predictive questions at all. The strongest counter-argument — drop molecular layers and work only on H&E or ctDNA — fails because ctDNA data are not public and because the molecular arm still offers mechanism and platform-robust features; the better design runs H&E as a parallel arm so the molecular features face the strongest public competitor.

## TCGA overall survival becomes one validation site among many

The TCGA Clinical Data Resource ranks the progression-free interval (PFI) as the most informative TCGA endpoint and recommends it without reservation for COAD and READ, while overall survival suffers from **median follow-up of only 22.0 months (COAD) and 20.0 months (READ)**; READ has just 26 OS events and "need[s] a longer follow-up", its disease-free interval (7 events) is not recommended, and OS counts non-cancer deaths ([Liu et al. 2018](https://escholarship.org/content/qt9ng938g3/qt9ng938g3.pdf)). Switching to PFI lifts events from 128 to about 162 across 629 patients, still far below what the Riley criteria demand for more than a handful of parameters. TCGA should therefore be one fold in an internal-external validation design, the home of the H&E arm, and the cohort that checks inferred miRNA activity against measured miRNA expression. The endpoint should be harmonised to 3-year RFS or time to recurrence, with competing non-cancer death handled explicitly through Fine–Gray or cause-specific hazards.

Because these cohorts span Affymetrix, Almac, RNA-seq and NanoString, successor features must be rank-based or single-sample scores — state fractions, program scores, CMS or iCMS calls — rather than raw gene weights that do not transfer across platforms. And because the single-cell atlases carry no survival follow-up, the split between feature definition and evaluation must be strict: atlases define features, bulk outcome cohorts only evaluate them.

Reproducibility also requires pinning every snapshot. The GDC changed TCGA data in place across releases DR42 to DR46 — DR42 exposed previously supplement-only clinical fields, DR43 moved survival-plot data back to `days_to_last_follow_up`, and DR46 removed MAFs built from contaminated alignments ([GDC release notes](https://docs.gdc.cancer.gov/Data/Release_Notes/Data_Release_Notes/)) — while TCGAbiolinks keeps hitting API breakages ([issue tracker](https://github.com/BioinformaticsFMRP/TCGAbiolinks/issues)). The recipe is to pin a GDC release (DR46, 2026-08-10) with file UUIDs and md5 sums committed in a manifest, cross-check against ISB-CGC's `TCGA_versioned` BigQuery tables, the only truly frozen, queryable TCGA snapshots found ([ISB-CGC](https://isb-cancer-genomics-cloud.readthedocs.io/en/latest/sections/data/TCGA_top.html)), pin CELLxGENE Census to the 2025-11-08 long-term-support build rather than "stable" ([Census releases](https://chanzuckerberg.github.io/cellxgene-census/cellxgene_census_docsite_data_release_info.html)), and pull CPTAC directly from the Proteomic Data Commons because the `cptac` Python package has not been released since 2024-06 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/cptac/)). The trial cohorts that matter most — PETACC-3, S:CORT, TransSCOT — sit behind consortium agreements, so those applications, not code, are the real critical path and should start in the first month.

## Patient-replicated cell states replace VIM-rooted pseudotime

GSE81861, with about 375 tumour cells from 11 patients on Fluidigm C1 reported as FPKM, is now roughly four orders of magnitude smaller than the integrated CRC reference of Marteau and colleagues: **4.27 million cells from 650 patients** and 1,670 samples, with 62 scANVI-labelled subtypes and states, released as h5ad files and in a cellxgene viewer ([GitHub icbi-lab/crc-atlas](https://github.com/icbi-lab/crc-atlas)), and extended in its Cancer Cell 2025 version with 3.7 million spatially profiled single cells ([Cancer Cell 2025](https://www.cell.com/cancer-cell/fulltext/S1535-6108(25)00537-9)). Around it sit Pelka's 371,223 cells split by MMR status ([SCP1162](https://singlecell.broadinstitute.org/single_cell/study/SCP1162)), Joanito's iCMS2/iCMS3 epithelial states from 63 patients ([Joanito 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-022-01100-4)), Lee's SMC and KUL3 cohorts ([Lee 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-020-0636-z)) and the HTAN polyp-to-CRC scATAC/scRNA data of Becker and colleagues, the main chromatin resource ([Becker 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-022-01088-x)). None carries survival follow-up, and the Marteau repository does not name its licence.

Three of the old steps fail on current evidence. Imputation introduces false-positive gene–gene correlations and differential-expression signals ([Andrews & Hemberg](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.16613.2)), and an evaluation of 18 imputation methods found limited, inconsistent downstream benefit ([Hou et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-020-02132-x)), which rules out MAGIC for statistics. The VIM root is biologically doubtful: the GSE81861 authors themselves reported that EMT genes were upregulated in cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), not in epithelial tumour cells ([Li et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3818)), and two independent studies showed that the poor-prognosis mesenchymal subtype is driven by stromal expression ([Isella 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3225); [Calon 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3224)), so a pseudotime rooted at low-VIM tumour cells leans on an axis whose bulk prognostic signal is mostly stroma. switchDE fits a per-cell sigmoid with no notion of samples, and methods that treat cells as replicates produce many false discoveries while pseudobulk methods agree best with ground truth ([Squair et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-25960-2)). RNA velocity is no rescue: across 15 methods and 17 datasets, agreement held only between certain method pairs and accuracy fell as dynamics grew complex ([velocity benchmark](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.02.668272v1.full)).

The decisive change is the unit of analysis, which becomes patient × cell state. scVI/scANVI latents, which rank well in the scIB integration benchmark ([Luecken 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-021-01336-8)), supply neighbourhoods and labels; pseudobulk or metacells supply expression; MAGIC survives only for figures. Features come from discrete states and programs with known outcome relevance: iCMS2/3, the EMP1-high "high-relapse cell" program that predicts relapse in human CRC cohorts ([Cañellas-Socias 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05402-9)), the FAP+ CAF / SPP1+ macrophage axis ([Qi 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-29366-6)) and pan-cancer meta-programs ([Gavish 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06130-4)). If a trajectory is kept, it should be CellRank 2 with CytoTRACE or pseudotime kernels on epithelial cells only, rooted on stem biology such as LGR5 rather than VIM ([CellRank 2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02303-9)), with dynamic genes tested by Lamian, which treats samples rather than cells as replicates ([Hou 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-42841-y)). The legacy VIM pseudotime should survive only as an ablation arm, so the paper can quantify what the redesign buys.

Carrying states into bulk is a deconvolution problem with a recent real-world answer. A 2026 benchmark on **18 real bulk cohorts (5,891 samples)** found ReCIDE and BayesPrism the most robust, found matrix CAFs prognostic across cancers, and warned that simulated-pseudobulk benchmarks "may not always be reliable" ([Genome Biology 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-026-03942-1)). BayesPrism estimates both fractions and cell-type-specific expression, including the malignant compartment ([Chu 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43018-022-00356-3)), which is the cleanest way to bring tumour-intrinsic single-cell signal into bulk *without* stromal confounding — the direct fix for the VIM problem. InstaPrism is fastest but weaker on real bulk ([Brief Bioinform 2026](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/27/2/bbag184/8659159)). Cell-prioritisation tools such as Scissor and scAB, rated most robust of eight methods in a preprint benchmark ([SigBridgeR](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.08.723458v1.full.pdf)), tune cell selection on the same bulk phenotype they are judged against, so they generate hypotheses only. Effects will be small: deconvolved immune states alone reached an AUC of only about 0.67–0.69 for CRC recurrence ([Kim et al. 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9636036/)). Single-cell foundation models have not earned a core role either — zero-shot scGPT and Geneformer embeddings performed inconsistently against HVG selection, scVI and Harmony ([Kedzierska 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-025-03574-x)), no perturbation model beat an additive baseline ([Ahlmann-Eltze 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12328236/)), a contested point disputed with calibrated metrics in a 2026 rebuttal ([Nat Biotechnol 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03307-w)), and no study shows their features improving bulk survival over clinical covariates — so they belong in an ablation arm with mandatory PCA and scVI comparators.

Spatial data validates mechanisms rather than supplying features. Sequencing-based CRC spatial studies remain small: Visium HD with five patients ([Oliveira 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40473992/)), CosMx with 846,469 cells from 23 samples and no outcome link ([Pattison preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.09.693138v1.full)), and Xenium-based STORM with 16 patients, whose "desmoplastic fibrotic barrier" reached **HR 1.50 only in a stage III/IV subset**, was not significant in the full TCGA cohort after FDR correction, and was neither adjusted for stage, MSI or CMS nor reported with a C-index ([STORM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13405321/)). Spatial data therefore has three jobs: confirming that candidate states co-localise in plausible niches, supplying niche signatures that can be projected onto bulk, and training classifiers that predict those signatures from H&E. Ligand–receptor inference annotates mechanism only, because methods and resources agree poorly ([Dimitrov 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-30755-0)), which is what motivates the LIANA+ consensus framework ([LIANA+](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-024-01469-w)). For paired multiome data, the 2025 multitask benchmark favours GLUE and MultiVI for integration and MOFA+ when feature selection is needed ([Nat Methods 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-025-02856-3)). Because standard 3′ droplet scRNA-seq does not measure mature miRNAs, any single-cell miRNA signal must arrive as inferred activity.

## Retire hoardeR and keep the miRNA idea as measured activity

hoardeR should not be ported. It has a single maintainer (Daniel Fischer, Luke), a GPL ≥2 licence and a last release of 0.10 on 2024-04-08, and CRAN **archived it on 2026-04-13** because "issues were not corrected despite reminders" ([CRAN](https://cran.r-project.org/package=hoardeR)) after installation failed on seven check flavours ([CRAN checks](https://cran-archive.r-project.org/web/checks/2026/2026-04-13_check_results_hoardeR.html)). Most of the package mines NCBI/BLAST across species; only `targetScan()` ever mattered to CcSinglecell, and it builds a URL with **`vert_71` hard-coded**, accepts a `release` argument it never uses, and splits the returned HTML on fixed markers into a conserved-site table ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fischuu/hoardeR/master/R/targetScan.R)). Tested on 7 November 2026, the vert_71 endpoint returns **HTTP 403** while vert_72 and vert_80 return full tables ([TargetScan 8.0 CGI](https://www.targetscan.org/cgi-bin/targetscan/vert_80/targetscan.cgi?species=Human&mirg=hsa-miR-21-5p)). That deserves a changelog line in the successor: the Cells 2023 methods name TargetScan 7.2, but hoardeR has only ever queried 7.1, so the published miRNA layer was very probably built on 7.1 web output. In the current package, hoardeR survives only under `inst/legacy`; the `R/` code no longer imports it.

Even a repaired scraper would be the wrong design, since it makes one HTTP request per miRNA, is tied to the web interface's default conserved-only view and records no release. TargetScan 8.0 publishes bulk files — `Summary_Counts.default_predictions.txt` and the context-score files — that download cleanly ([TargetScan 8.0](https://www.targetscan.org/vert_80/)); filtering by miRNA family and keeping the ten rows with the lowest cumulative weighted context++ reproduces CcSinglecell's `maxOut = 10` exactly, offline. "Improving hoardeR" should therefore mean a loader of about 50 lines inside the new repository that takes a source name and release, downloads at runtime rather than shipping third-party tables (licence terms were not verified), records URL, version and checksum in the data manifest, and returns one long table tagged with its evidence class. The obvious shortcuts fall short: targetscan.Hs.eg.db is no longer in Bioconductor release, and multiMiR bundles a 2024-08-28 snapshot whose miRTarBase is the 2021 version and queries a remote server, making it a convenience rather than a reproducibility boundary ([multiMiR](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/vignettes/multiMiR/inst/doc/multiMiR.html)).

That loader should serve pluggable sources grouped by evidence class. For prediction, TargetScan 8 context++, released with McGeary and colleagues ([McGeary 2022](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.69803)), sits beside scanMiR KdModels in Bioconductor 3.23 ([scanMiR](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/scanMiR.html)), which build on the biochemical affinity model that predicted repression better than TargetScan 7 context++ ([McGeary 2019](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aav1741)). For experimental support, TarBase v9 offers about 6 million entries annotated across 172 tissues and cell types, including AGO-CLIP ([TarBase v9](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/52/D1/D304/7438911)), and miRTarBase 2025 offers more than 3.8 million validated interactions with LLM-assisted curation ([miRTarBase 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/11701613)). Join keys should be miRBase v22 accessions plus MirGeneDB 3.0 families (21,822 genes in 1,743 families) ([MirGeneDB 3.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39673268/)), with legacy dbDEMC names translated by miRBaseConverter ([miRBaseConverter](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/miRBaseConverter.html)). Deep-learning predictors do not justify a swap: miRBench showed that negative-sampling bias inflates reported performance ([miRBench](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12261448/)); the best 2026 model, miRBind2, beats TargetScan 8 on gene-level repression by only **Pearson 0.30 against 0.24**, on 7 miRNAs from the same source as its training data ([miRBind2](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/42/Supplement_2/btag410/8767265)); and an expression-anchored comparison concluded that "no single tool is optimal" ([Jin et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.33960)). With repression predictable to about r = 0.3 at best, effort belongs in activity, not predictor swaps; deep models can be an optional backend, and every ranking should report its sensitivity to the target source.

Activity should replace correlation. The current `calculate_mirna_activity()` measures gene–miRNA anti-correlation in TCGA, the weakest-evidenced design available: across 11 TCGA cancers, miRNA–target correlations averaged **−0.38 to −0.61 in normal tissue but only −0.04 to −0.26 in tumours** ([PMC6304515](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6304515/)), and tumour purity confounds correlation analyses across 21 cancer types ([Aran et al. 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms9971)). The field has moved to motif and target-set enrichment over the transcriptome, which needs only mRNA data: miReact; miTEA-HiRes, which covers single-cell and spatial data ([Commun Biol 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-07454-9)); and bayesReact, stronger on sparse and single-cell data while miReact is slightly better on full bulk, whose authors caution that activity inference remains "challenging to benchmark" ([NAR 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12884093/)). The cleanest architecture is one **regulator-activity module** on decoupler that scores signed, weighted miRNA→target networks (weight −context++ or −log Kd), CollecTRI TF regulons ([Müller-Dott 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkad841)) and PROGENy pathway footprints ([Schubert 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-02391-6)) through a single API ([decoupleR](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/decoupleR.html)), per patient × state pseudobulk and on bulk cohorts. Activity scores, not row-sum target counts — which mostly reward genes with long, site-rich 3′UTRs whether or not their miRNAs are active — should feed the survival model.

The miRNA list itself needs rethinking. dbDEMC encodes differential expression, which is mostly diagnostic evidence, and even diagnostic circulating-miRNA panels, at a pooled AUC of 0.90, mostly lack external validation ([meta-analysis 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12578759/)); prognostic support is thinner still. The successor should select miRNAs by their activity and variability in the actual data and keep the dbDEMC list as a legacy ablation. The honest expectation is that the miRNA layer's main value is mechanistic — which cell compartment a regulatory program acts in — so it should be tested as one block in the survival model rather than assumed predictive. No published study shows that this design improves CRC prognosis.

## Clinical-favouring penalties and pre-registered nulls beat blended rankings

Blending per-gene rankings with grid, PCA, DIABLO or MOFA weights *before* fitting has no benchmark support; every benchmarked integrator either applies group-wise penalties, splits on groups or fuses learned representations, and respecting group structure while favouring clinical variables helped on average ([Herrmann 2021](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/22/3/bbaa167/5895463)). The modelling arm should therefore run clinical-only Cox as the reference, then priority-Lasso with an unpenalised clinical block, CoxBoost with mandatory clinical covariates, IPF-Lasso, and block forest (best mean C in Herrmann, best integrated Brier score in SurvBoard), with elastic-net Cox retained for continuity and cooperative learning as an optional dial between early and late fusion whose survival evidence is thin and comes from outside cancer ([Ding et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.12337)). MOFA+ and DIABLO are demoted to exploration: in a cell-line benchmark, MOFA2 and the iCluster family "did not perform better than PCA" ([Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/genetics/articles/10.3389/fgene.2022.854752/full)), DIABLO is a classifier, and practical guidance prefers MOFA once missingness across layers exceeds about 30% ([STAR Protocols 2026](https://www.cell.com/star-protocols/fulltext/S2666-1667(26)00187-5)). Their factors can still enter as candidate covariates fitted inside cross-validation folds. Pan-cancer pretraining and training on samples with missing modalities are the two tricks with benchmark-level support in SurvBoard.

The evidence argues against the current roadmap's Phase 5 autoencoder. "Sparsity is all you need" found that **randomly rewired networks of equal sparsity matched pathway-informed models** (randomised P-NET AUC 0.896 against 0.899) ([Brief Bioinform 2026](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/27/4/bbag425/8753615)); a calibration audit of multimodal survival models found **166 of 290 tests rejecting calibration**, most of it repairable by Platt scaling ([arXiv 2604.04239](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04239)); and the SurvPath authors concede that a simple transcriptomics MLP beats several multimodal methods ([SurvPath](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.06819v2)). Any neural arm needs the same tuning budget as elastic-net and block forest, evaluation on integrated Brier score and calibration, and a random-wiring control. The H&E arm should use frozen foundation-model features with attention-based multiple-instance learning, fused late as an offset to the clinical linear predictor and then Platt-recalibrated, because in TCGA CRC unimodal histology (0.728) beat the multimodal MMP model (0.636) and in PathBench no encoder led every CRC survival task ([PathBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20202)). BulkFormer's survival claims rest on an abstract whose numbers contradict its text and which reports no raw-expression baseline ([AACR 2026](https://aacr2026-wiki.vercel.app/references/5261)), so it is one more block to test. LLM agents are "more reliable as assistants than fully autonomous agents" ([BAISBench](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/42/Supplement_1/btag227/8726318)); their documented weakness at choosing methods is exactly what produces optimistic signatures, so they never pick biomarkers.

Validation statistics need four upgrades. First, every data-driven selection step — differential-expression filtering, state choice, factor fitting and the elastic-net λ — moves inside the outer folds. Second, feature lists get error control from stability selection or derandomised knockoffs adapted to Cox regression, which control the k-family-wise error rate ([arXiv 2504.03907](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03907v2)), followed by a low-dimensional refit within the Riley parameter budget. Third, the existing genomic-block permutation test is joined by Venet-style size-matched random and proliferation-matched nulls, and conformalised survival analysis adds distribution-free, doubly robust lower bounds on time to recurrence for each patient ([Candès et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09763v3)), whose coverage in each external cohort doubles as a transportability check. Fourth, attributions come from SurvSHAP(t) with stability checks across resamples ([SurvSHAP(t)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11080)), and attention maps from multiple-instance models are never reported as biology without perturbation-based faithfulness tests ([Albuquerque 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02484)).

Reporting follows TRIPOD+AI, a 27-item checklist that supersedes TRIPOD 2015 ([Collins 2024](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/116670/1/bmj-2023-078378.full.pdf)), PROBAST+AI for risk of bias ([Moons 2025](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:8b15c087-459b-43fa-ad03-1f66ed6ba370)) and the BMJ 2024 evaluation series for external-validation design ([part 1](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/files/223789914/bmj-2023-074819.full.pdf)). The CcSinglecell harness already reports bootstrap ΔC, repeated CV, a calibration table and a power curve. What it lacks is a pmsampsize gate before fitting, internal-external CV across cohorts instead of random folds, time-dependent AUC and integrated Brier score, calibration slope and ICI at 3 years, decision-curve net benefit at a clinically defined threshold such as the 3-year recurrence risk at which oxaliplatin would be withheld ([dcurves](https://cloud.r-project.org/web/packages/dcurves/refman/dcurves.html)), and a TRIPOD+AI and PROBAST+AI checklist shipped with each results release.

## A two-language stack that exchanges files, not processes

Single-cell and spatial analysis now centres on Python and scverse. anndata 0.12 added Zarr v3 and lazy loading ([scverse blog](https://scverse.org/blog/2025-anndata-012/)) and 0.13 moved X into layers with Zarr v3 as default ([release notes](https://anndata.scverse.org/en/stable/release-notes/)); scvi-tools 1.5 added rapids-singlecell support ([changelog](https://docs.scvi-tools.org/en/latest/changelog.html)); decoupler 2.x is an scverse package that LIANA+ 1.7.3 requires ([LIANA+](https://liana-py.readthedocs.io/en/latest/changelog.html)); and HTAN delivers single-cell files as h5ad following the CELLxGENE schema, checked by a validator ([HTAN](https://docs.humantumoratlas.org/data_submission/scrnaseq_data_submission/)). R stays where the survival statistics live — Bioconductor 3.23 on R 4.6 ([Bioconductor](https://bioconductor.org/about/release-announcements)) — for priority-Lasso, blockForest, pmsampsize, dcurves and glmnet. The two meet through files rather than embedded interpreters: anndataR reads and writes H5AD and Zarr natively without Python ([anndataR 2026](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/42/6/btag288/8675377)), zellkonverter itself now recommends it ([zellkonverter](https://github.com/theislab/zellkonverter/releases)), and MOFA runs entirely in Python on MuData through muon and mofapy2 ([muon](https://muon.readthedocs.io/en/stable/changelog.html)), eliminating the reticulate bridge `integrate_mofa()` needs today. The repo should pin one anndata minor line and round-trip a fixture through anndataR in CI, since the 0.12-to-0.13 change is the likeliest silent breakage.

Pixi locks conda-forge, bioconda and PyPI dependencies (the latter through uv) in a single committed lockfile ([pixi](https://pixi.prefix.dev/latest/switching_from/uv/)), with `default`, `r` and `gpu` environments; whether Bioconductor packages resolve cleanly under pixi is unverified and should be tested in week one, keeping renv only for CRAN- or GitHub-only packages. Snakemake 9 (9.27.0, September 2026) orchestrates the analysis layer ([changelog](https://snakemake.readthedocs.io/en/stable/project_info/history.html)), and any raw reprocessing uses unmodified nf-core pipelines at pinned revisions; a team writing its own Nextflow should start on strict syntax, the default since 26.04 and required by nf-core/tools 4.0.0 at Nextflow ≥25.10.4 ([nf-core tools 4.0](https://nf-co.re/blog/2026/tools-4_0_0)). Nested resampling runs in mlr3proba ([mlr3proba](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.08080)) or scikit-survival pipelines ([scikit-survival](https://github.com/sebp/scikit-survival)), with TorchSurv for any neural ablation ([TorchSurv](https://theoj.org/joss-papers/joss.07341/10.21105.joss.07341.pdf)). MLflow 3 with a local store logs parameters, fold IDs, metrics and the manifest hash ([MLflow 3](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/genai/mlflow-3)); because DVC passed to lakeFS in November 2025 ([DVC](https://dvc.org/blog/dvc-joins-lakefs-your-questions-answered/)), checksummed manifests plus Zenodo deposits are the lighter, vendor-neutral choice.

GPUs are needed only for whole-slide-image features and, optionally, scVI; rapids-singlecell 0.15 now installs as `-cu12` or `-cu13` wheels ([scverse blog](https://scverse.org/blog/2026-rsc-goes-nanobind/)). Licensing shapes the H&E arm: UNI2-h and Virchow2 are gated under **CC-BY-NC-ND 4.0, with restrictions extending to derived models** ([UNI2-h](https://huggingface.co/MahmoodLab/UNI2-h); [Virchow2](https://huggingface.co/paige-ai/Virchow2/blob/main/README.md)), so the arm runs on a user-supplied HuggingFace token, caches embeddings rather than weights, and ships a permissively licensed fallback encoder so CI runs end to end (which open encoder to use was not researched). CITATION.cff plus the GitHub-to-Zenodo integration mints a DOI for every release, nf-prov 1.8.0 emits Workflow Run RO-Crate provenance from Nextflow ([nf-prov](https://registry.nextflow.io/plugins/nf-prov)) — a Snakemake equivalent was not verified — and agents stay outside the pipeline path, not least because Biomni warns that its generated code runs with full system privileges ([Biomni](https://github.com/snap-stanford/Biomni)).

The sequencing is deliberate. Phase 0 turns the old null into a regression test, so any later change that "finds" signal can be traced to its cause. Phase 1 locks endpoints and baselines before any new feature exists, the cheapest protection against the forking-paths version of Venet's problem, and starts the consortium applications that dominate the timeline. Once the evaluation frame is fixed, Phases 2, 3 and 5 run in parallel, and optional arms — scGPT, Geneformer or BulkFormer embeddings, a deep survival head, the legacy MAGIC/VIM/dbDEMC features — join Phase 4 as ablation blocks, never as defaults. Two items in the existing roadmap should be rewritten: its Phase 5 autoencoder-plus-SHAP is dropped on the sparsity and calibration evidence, and its Phase 6 multi-omic pseudotime becomes multiome *annotation* of the patient-level states with GLUE, MultiVI or MOFA+. The gates assume the most likely molecular outcome is still a null, so each phase yields a publishable, bounded result on its own.

## Conclusion

The scarce resource has flipped. Integration methods, foundation models and agent frameworks are commodities that benchmarks repeatedly show add little, while outcome-annotated cohorts with decision-relevant endpoints, and the access agreements behind them, are the real bottleneck — which puts the successor's critical path in consortium applications and endpoint harmonisation rather than in code. Its most defensible contribution, whichever way the data fall, is a pre-registered, cross-cohort evaluation frame that converts any ΔC into a bounded claim, together with cell-state features that survive platform changes; a new signature is not the goal.

The hoardeR episode is a provenance lesson in miniature: a silently versioned web endpoint left the published miRNA layer very likely on TargetScan 7.1 under a 7.2 label, and then the endpoint died. In the successor, every external resource enters through a manifest with a version and a checksum. The miRNA layer's realistic role also shifts from predictor to annotator, showing which compartment a regulatory program acts in. If the successor does find a positive ΔC, the evidence here says it will most likely come from malignant-compartment state expression or H&E features measured against stage plus MMR, not from miRNA target counts — a prediction the pre-registration should state up front so the result can falsify it.
